## Content
Data collection has been performed on a country-by-country basis and systematically structured within both the residential and service sectors. This organized methodology ensures that specific building categories and individual construction periods are comprehensively considered and properly represented.
The residential sector has been divided into the following building typologies:
-	Single family houses (including terraced houses) (SFHs); 
-	Multifamily houses (MFHs); Apartment blocks (ABs – high-rise buildings containing multiple dwellings and exceeding four storeys in height) (Hotmaps, 2018).
The service sector includes the following categories:
-	Offices: this category covers both public and private office buildings, including office complexes. 
-	Trade: it includes different types of establishments such as standalone shops, department stores, shopping malls, grocery stores, vehicle dealerships and garages, bakeries, hairdressers, service stations, laundries, conference and exhibition facilities, and other wholesale and retail infrastructures. 
-	Education: this category comprises primary, secondary, and high schools, universities, vocational training facilities, student dormitories, and research centres/laboratories. 
-	Health: it includes public and private hospitals, nursing homes, and healthcare centres. 
-	Hotels and restaurants: this category covers hotels, hostels, cafés, pubs, restaurants, canteens, and catering facilities for companies. 
-	Other non-residential buildings: this category consists of warehouses, transport and garage buildings, military facilities, agricultural buildings such as farms and greenhouses, together with sports infrastructures including sports halls, swimming pools, and fitness centres (Buildings Performance Institute Europe, 2011). 

To provide a complete overview of the building stock among MSs and to describe time-related characteristics, the following construction periods have been established:
-	Before 1945: buildings constructed before 1945 are classified as historic buildings. The historic building stock is highly heterogeneous, making standardized assessments difficult. Nevertheless, a number of common characteristics can be identified, such as the widespread use of solid construction techniques in residential buildings. 
-	1945-1969: buildings erected after World War II and before 1969 are often characterized by insufficient insulation and inefficient energy systems. This is mainly linked to the use of low-cost construction materials and rapid construction practices. As a result, these buildings typically present high specific energy demands. 
-	1970-1979: buildings constructed between 1970 and 1979 reflect the introduction of the first insulation measures, mainly driven by the global energy crises of the 1970s. 
-	1980-1989 and 1990-1999: buildings built during these periods correspond to the implementation of the first national thermal efficiency regulations, which began to emerge around 1990. 
-	2000-2010: buildings from this period were strongly influenced by the EU Energy Performance of Buildings Directive – EPBD (2002/91/EC and its subsequent revisions), and their analysis contributes to understanding its impact. 
-	2011-2020: recently constructed buildings are analysed to assess the influence of the economic crisis on the European construction sector. 
-	2020-2023: buildings constructed during this period reflect the increasing adoption of nearly zero-energy building principles, stricter decarbonisation targets, and the accelerated integration of renewable energy technologies and high-efficiency systems promoted by updated EU climate and energy policies.
